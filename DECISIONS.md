# DECISIONS (unattended night run, 11–12 Oct 2026)

Each line: decision, and why. Newest at the bottom.

1. **Telegram login:** phone + code + 2FA typed by Dexter once; session saved in `tools/course-ingest/.state/` (git-ignored). Telethon is used read-only (iter_messages, download_media, get_dialogs); nothing is sent, joined, marked read or deleted.
2. **Course scope:** the 13 chats in the Telegram folder IELTS STUFF (found through the folder filter, not by name matching). The separate "Speaking" and "Free courses" folders were NOT touched, as instructed ("everything in that folder").
3. **Python 3.11 venv** for the tools (`tools/course-ingest/.venv`): faster-whisper/ctranslate2 wheels are reliable there; Python 3.14 is the system default.
4. **Downloads:** ~29 GB in total at ~1 MB/s on this connection, so order is: documents/photos, audio, then videos shortest first. Long videos come last and may not finish by morning (listed in MORNING.md).
5. **Transcription:** faster-whisper `small`, int8, CPU, 8 threads, VAD on, beam 1. Audio first (shortest first), then videos shortest first, videos > 40 min skipped.
6. **Course content stays private.** Hard limit "never commit course files" + brief section 12 ("no public links, no re-sharing") beats "course media goes into the build": the public GitHub Pages app contains only the plan's own content. All course-derived data (catalogue, day materials, vocabulary cards, video list) is a single **course pack** file (`course/eight-course-pack.json`, git-ignored) that Dexter sends to her and she loads in Settings > Course pack (stays in her browser's IndexedDB). A private local build with the pack baked in is in `build-private/` (git-ignored).
7. **Stack:** plain HTML/CSS/ES modules, no framework, no bundler for app code (Dexter wants readable code, no magic). `ts-fsrs` is bundled once with esbuild into `app/vendor/ts-fsrs.mjs`. Data in IndexedDB through a tiny hand-written wrapper (no Dexie).
8. **Hash routing** (`#/today`) so GitHub Pages needs no server rewrites; every asset path is relative so it works at `/eight/`.
9. **Her name:** unknown (open question 1 in the brief) → the first-launch welcome asks "What should I call you?" and stores it on her device only.
10. **Lesson time and meeting link:** unknown → Settings fields (default time 19:00, Asia/Tashkent, no link). Countdown and Join button work once she or Dexter sets them.
11. **Design direction:** Rishtan ceramics (the brief's suggested direction): cobalt + turquoise + warm white on an ink-dark night theme, one ikat-derived pattern used only in the welcome and checkpoint moments.
12. **Network reality:** this PC downloads at ~0.2–1 MB/s, so 29 GB could not finish overnight. Download order became: documents/photos under 100 MB → voice and audio under 40 MB → videos up to 40 min (smallest first) → everything else. 12 parallel workers were slower than 4, so the pool is 4.
13. **Fonts:** Bricolage Grotesque (display, band numbers, timers: tabular figures) and Instrument Sans (text, tabular figures). Both are free (OFL), self-hosted as woff2, so the app works offline. Neither is on the usual "default" list; Bricolage has a slightly irregular, warm character that suits a one-person gift, Instrument Sans stays quiet next to it.
14. **Colour:** light = warm white `#F5F3EE`, cobalt `#1E3FA8`, turquoise `#0A726E`; dark = ink `#0B1120`, soft cobalt `#8FAEFF`, turquoise `#3ECFC8`. Five pattern colours (Task, Article, Collocation, Grammar, Template) each with a shape (square, circle, triangle, diamond, cross) so colour never works alone. axe-core: 0 moderate-or-worse issues on 15 screens × 2 themes × 2 sizes.
15. **Signature element:** the overall band estimate set large on Today and Progress, with the distance to 8. Until speaking is scored (Lesson 1) it is assumed equal to writing, and the screen says so.
16. **Evidence rules:** writing and speaking tasks complete only when an essay is submitted or a recording is kept (a bare tap refuses, except on a minimum day); reading, fix, error-log and listening tasks complete with a tap. Reviews and reading logs also count.
17. **No AI, no marking in the app.** Bands for essays and speaking are typed in by her after Dexter marks them (Essay page → Band from your teacher; Progress → Add a result). That keeps "teacher's band is the official one".
18. **Speaking banks:** cue cards are the 29 named in the plan; the "You should say" lines, Part 1 and Part 3 question sets are generic fallbacks I wrote. Where the private course pack is loaded, the course's own questions and cue cards come first.
19. **Course videos on Today** ("Watch tonight"): for each study day, up to two videos on what was taught the day before (≤ 30 min, from Ultimate Task 1/2 and Reading Question Types), plus one reading-analysis marathon walkthrough (≤ 45 min), alternating RM 11&12 and RM 13 in marathon order. They open in Telegram (`t.me/c/...`), which works for members of those channels.
20. **Part 3 / vocabulary cards from the course** go into a Card inbox (nothing joins her deck unreviewed): 318 vocabulary entries from wordlists and ~700 "useful chunks" and phrase entries.
21. **Student feedback videos in Writing group G8 show other students' names**: the catalogue replaces those titles and they are never suggested.
22. **Public repo content:** the plan's own content, including the 29 error-log rows from her mock (her sentences, no name). That is personal-ish; the brief wants it seeded. Dexter can swap the repo to private (Pages then needs a paid plan) or remove `errors` from `app/data/plan.json` if he prefers.
23. **Hosting:** GitHub Pages from branch `gh-pages` of `iamdexterdude/eight`; source on `main`. The staging copy is in `deploy/` (git-ignored by the outer repo).
24. **Renamed to 8.5 on request** (she asked for this band): app name, title, icon, manifest, welcome, calendar file, and the GitHub repo `iamdexterdude/8.5` (Pages URL is now `/8.5/`; the old `/eight/` link is dead). Internal ids stay `eight` (database name, backup format, course pack kind) so existing data and files keep working.
25. **Targets:** overall 8.5; per-skill aims listening 9, reading 9, writing 8, speaking 8 (average 8.5), shown as markers on the band bars. The plan document itself is unchanged (it still describes 8 and 8.5 routes; routes D and E are tagged 8.5).
26. **Teacher visibility without a backend:** a self-contained HTML report built on her device and shared by her (Web Share where supported, else a file download). Chosen over a free backend because it needs no accounts, keeps her essays and recordings off third-party servers, and works today. Recordings are opt-in because they make the file large. The report is read-only: Dexter's marks still go back to her by typing bands (essay page, Progress > Add a result).
27. **Bug found by testing and fixed:** typing a name then tapping Skip in the welcome lost the name.
28. **Course decks (Anki-style), built on request, all defaults:** every model answer and word list becomes a **named deck** (for example "Speaking Part 1: Having a Day Off", "Speaking Part 2: A friend of yours who is good at music/singing", "Speaking Part 3: Work", "Reading: Day 4, Passage 3, A Prehistoric Took to the Skies", "Reading: Article 4"), plus "My mistakes" and "Cards I added". 99 decks, 1,708 cards. A deck's cards join her deck, all at once and with **no daily limit**, on the day the deck is assigned; scheduling is FSRS as before. Cards come from the course's own "phrase – meaning / example" lists, the highlighted phrases in the Part 3 model answers, and the reading marathon word lists.
29. **Course homework in the plan (private pack only):** per day, "Read and listen to the model answer" tasks (Part 1, Part 2, Part 3) and one "Learn today's new words" task. The Part 1 questions for the topic are listed, the model answer text is readable in the app, with links to the voice model and PDF in Telegram. Reading: the marathon passage of the day, its notes and walkthrough video, and its word decks. The walkthrough video moved to "Watch tonight" for the same day.
30. **Matching rules:** Part 1 uses the topics named in the plan's warm-ups (with synonyms) first, then the course's next topic; Part 2 uses the closest model answer for the day's cue card, or the next model answer of the same kind, labelled as the closest; Part 3 uses one topic a day from the 12 in the course; reading follows the marathon's own day order. Some course PDFs are scanned images (no text), so a few topics have questions but no word deck.
31. **8.5 Course Library (13 Oct, on request):** one private Telegram channel on Dexter's account (id 4384453771, no public username, only invited members can see it). The 5 course chats that allow forwarding were forwarded in their original order with a header post per course (1,015 posts, done in about two minutes on Telegram's servers, author names hidden): RM 13, LM 1.0, LM 3.0, Transcribing channel, Writing G8. The 8 chats whose owner turned on "Restrict saving content" (Reading Question Types, RM 11&12, Listening Question Types, Ultimate Task 1, Ultimate Task 2, Diyorbek's Writing, Gold Plan, USB) were NOT copied: re-uploading them would get around a protection the author set. A public channel with the original names was declined (it would redistribute a paid course and look like the author's own). Map of copied posts: `course/mirror_map.json`; script: `tools/course-ingest/mirror.py`.
32. **Today redesigned (13 Oct, from Dexter's sketch):** the big band score is gone from Today (it stays on Progress). Today = the day, the lesson and what it covers (with countdown and Join), the previous homework status, a note "what I learnt" on lesson days, then the teacher-set homework, then one quiet line for cards ready. The month calendar stays on the right on wide screens. The plan's hard-coded task lists no longer drive Today; the Plan tab stays as the outline.
33. **Teacher-set, flexible homework:** each day's homework is a JSON file of tasks, each a list of typed steps (questions, read a sample answer with highlighted phrases, new words that become a named deck, watch a link, photos made into one PDF, one recording per question, check, note). She does the steps one by one; each finished step collapses and the next opens; when all are done a finish screen appears. With nothing published she sees "No homework yet" and can ask (Telegram message to the teacher).
34. **Free delivery with no server:** homework and class settings are **encrypted** (AES-GCM, key from a class code through PBKDF2) and committed to a `data` branch of the public repo; her app fetches `raw.githubusercontent.com` and decrypts. The public repo therefore never holds readable course text or the teacher's bot token. The class code travels only in the join link (`#/join/<code>`, a URL fragment, never sent to GitHub). Verified for real: a stranger sees ciphertext, the app with the code reads it, a wrong code is refused.
35. **Her work reaches the teacher on Telegram** through a bot Dexter creates (token and chat id inside the encrypted settings): PDFs and recordings are posted as documents named like `2026-10-13_speaking-written-answers.pdf` and `..._Q1.webm`. If the bot is not set up or she is offline, files wait in an outbox on her device and are sent later. Telegram Bot API allows browser requests (CORS `*`), checked with curl.
36. **Teacher mode** (`#/admin`, Settings > Teacher mode): a teacher code keeps students out of the screen (a UI lock, not security); real protection is that publishing needs Dexter's GitHub token, kept on his device only. Homework can also be sent to Claude in chat and published with `node tools/teacher/publish.mjs homework <file>`; the class code is in `tools/teacher/.classcode` (not committed).
37. **The finish screen:** a woven strip of ikat medallions, the time she finished in large figures, one plain line that depends on the hour (a different line after 23:00), and one track from the teacher's list (link only, no audio is stored). No confetti, emoji or exclamation marks.
38. **The student types nothing; the teacher types one password (14 Oct, Dexter: "student doesn't need to enter access code … only password I set").** The class code now reaches her only through her join link (`#/join/<code>`), once; the Today box for typing a code is gone (plain URL says "Open the link from your teacher"). The feed moved to its own public repo, `iamdexterdude/8.5-feed` (branch `main`), so the app repo holds no course data at all. Teacher mode no longer asks for a GitHub token each time: a fine-grained token limited to the feed repo (Contents read/write) is pasted once at setup and kept in `vault.json` in the feed repo, encrypted with the teacher's password (AES-GCM, PBKDF2 600,000 iterations, password 10+ characters). Afterwards the password alone unlocks it on any device; the unlocked key lives only in `sessionStorage`. Honest limit: publishing from a browser needs some write credential, and a vault anyone can download is only as strong as the password. Not done on purpose: embedding the class code or a token in the app (it would make the homework effectively public).
39. **One link, one password (14 Oct, Dexter: "only one link, that's it", "don't ask for the GitHub token, only the password").** The class code, the join route and the repair page are gone. Her link is the plain site URL; the feed files are scrambled with a key that is built into the app (so the public repo is not readable at a glance, but it is not a secret). Publishing and her uploads go through one free Vercel function (`backend/`) that holds the GitHub token and the Telegram bot token; the app holds neither. Teacher mode asks for the password only; the relay checks it against a salted PBKDF2 hash in `teacher.json` (600k iterations, 10+ characters). Her uploads arrive in Dexter's Telegram chat with the Claude Code bot (no BotFather step for him); a dedicated bot can be set in the Vercel env later. Her name comes from the published class settings, so the welcome asks nothing.
40. **The "name asked every time" and "not being saved" reports:** could not be reproduced in a fresh browser, so the storage layer was hardened instead of guessed at: each database opens at whatever version it has (no VersionError path), settings are mirrored in localStorage and merged back, writes made while a database is not open are kept and written when it opens, a closed connection reconnects on its own, and a store that fails three times with UnknownError is rebuilt. The red banner was removed; Settings shows one quiet line if saving is not working. The service worker is cache-first again (instant start, offline), with an update check on every open and when the app comes back to the foreground; a new version takes over and reloads once.
41. **Tell Claude:** a teacher-mode tab where Dexter writes the homework in words. It opens Telegram with the text addressed to the Claude Code bot, copies it for the chat, or saves it (scrambled) in the feed under `requests/` for `publish.mjs requests`. No AI call from the app: everything stays free.
42. **The 25 Oct layout (Dexter's spec): five tabs, Today · Homework · Mock · Anki · Progress.** Plan, Practice, Review, the band calculator, the HTML report, the error log, the timer and the course-work screens are gone (their files deleted, old device data cleared once by a migration). Today carries no homework any more: the greeting, the lesson, the teacher's note, one or two check-in questions and the week dots. Settings is reduced to name, theme, lesson time and link, install, backup, Teacher link.
43. **Check-ins are a question bank, not one question:** keyed prompts with the phase they fit (lesson day, soon, during, after, none) and the hours they suit; at most two a day; the last 14 keys are avoided so the same question does not come back for two weeks; after a lesson they ask what to keep, what was unclear, the pace, what she wishes to learn, what the app should change. Answers go to Telegram as one line and into the small report. Push notifications were not built (GitHub Pages has no push server; a free one would add an account); the Homework tab's periodic nudge covers "something is waiting".
44. **Mocks are an in-app full-screen route (`#/exam/<id>/<part>`), not a new tab:** a new tab cannot be opened reliably from a phone PWA, and a same-document route lets the app keep the clock, the answers and the leave count on the device. The screen copies the computer-delivered IELTS: black bar with IELTS, candidate name, clock with Hide, Screen (fullscreen), the 10- and 5-minute notices, the question palette with answered and Review states, the passage on the left with a draggable split and a highlighter, the essay editor with live word count and no spell-check, the recording that plays once and then two minutes to check. Leaving is counted from `visibilitychange`, `blur` and `fullscreenchange` and reported with an encouraging line, never a punishment. A part whose clock ran out while the screen was closed is handed in as it is. Parts must be done in order. Fullscreen is requested with a 1.5 s cap because some browsers neither grant nor refuse it.
45. **Marking on the device for Listening and Reading** (normalised answers, every accepted form from the key, "(the) x" and "a/b" forms, multi-letter groups counted per letter, a shorter test scaled to 40 before the band table), Writing texts and Speaking recordings to Telegram with the teacher's estimates published back as `results/<id>.json`. The listening audio is published from the PC as scrambled 4 MB pieces (`mocks/<id>/audio-N.<k>.json`) because the relay cannot carry large bodies; the app stitches and caches them in IndexedDB. Test mocks on a device: a `localStorage` key `eight-mock-<id>` holding the mock JSON is merged into the Mock list and used by the exam, which is how the engine was tested without the feed.
46. **Anki starts from zero and belongs to her:** no card is ever added without her seeing it; her own decks and cards with full control; teacher decks (from a lesson's words step, from Teacher mode's quick deck, or from Send to Claude, kind Deck) arrive as named decks she can also delete (remembered, so they do not come back). The teacher's Anki note is one quiet line at the top, shown once.
47. **The teacher sees her work through a small plain report** (`students/<id>.json`, written by the relay's `report` op, at most once a minute, 200 KB cap): Anki stats, finished mock parts with bands and leaves, homework days, the last check-in answers, the streak. Plain JSON, not scrambled, because it holds nothing but her first name and numbers, and Teacher mode must read it on any device without the key.
48. **Send to Claude has kinds (homework, mock, deck):** the watcher picks the skill (`publish-homework`, `publish-mock`, `publish-deck`), the output file and the check command by kind; a mock's audio files are copied next to its `mock.json` and published as pieces; the request's context lists the published mocks and decks and the next mock id.
49. **Found by the real click-through, fixed:** the question palette object was destructured wrongly, so every Listening and Reading screen crashed on open; the screen swap depended on the browser painting a frame (a hidden tab or a background page never showed the new screen), so the swap now happens anyway after 400 ms and an older render can never draw over a newer one; the Writing task tabs now show the live word count.
50. **Today has a pulse (25 Oct, Dexter: "a little lock-in doesn't hurt"):** `app/js/pulse.js` reads what is already on the device (visits, homework completion times, mock finishes, check-ins, the review log) and gives Today three things. *The next small thing*: what is waiting, in priority order (today's homework, yesterday's, the unfinished mock, the cards due) or, when nothing is, one small idea seeded by the date (three new cards, a run through a deck she knows, the last sample answer again, her own Part 2 recording), with a Sunday kicker that keeps the "nothing required" promise while still offering ten minutes. *One observation*: a true, kind sentence about her days (past ten now; late nights three times this week; homework finished after eleven; homework days late; the chain about to break; days away; early today; a steady week; Monday's numbers), each with a one-tap reply that goes to the teacher as a check-in, or "Let it go"; an answered or dismissed observation stays away for three days. *The week in numbers* next to the dots. Visits are the only new record (one per 20 minutes, the last 100). The same habit numbers travel in the student report and appear under Her work. The ikat from the welcome drifts behind the sky and marks the next-step card, so the first screen and the daily screen share one signature.
