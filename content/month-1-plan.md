# IELTS 8 — Month 1 Plan

> Exported from the Claude Doc "IELTS 8 — Month 1 Plan" on 11 Oct 2026. The doc is the source of truth: when it changes, re-export it and re-run the importer. The roadmap diagram from the doc is rendered here as a table.

Month 1 of a two-to-three-month plan to overall 8 or higher. Speaking comes first, writing second, reading third; listening is already at target.

## Starting point

Listening is already at target. Writing (6.5) and reading (7.5) carry the gap, and speaking — the top priority — has no score yet.

| Skill | Mock result | Band | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listening | 37/40 | 8.5 | Hold |
| Reading | 33/40 | 7.5 | 9 |
| Writing | Task 1: 6 · Task 2: 6.5 | 6.5 | 7.5–8 |
| Speaking | Not tested yet | Set in Lesson 1 | 7.5–8 |

**Writing.** Your grammar range is already at 7 or above: passives, conditionals, participle clauses, concessions. What costs marks is accuracy and task handling, which are faster to fix than range.

- **Task 1:** the overview doesn't name the main changes, parts of the plans are misreported or missing, and the paragraphs follow compass points that don't match their content.
- **Task 2:** no conclusion; the first body paragraph defends summer holidays in general, not long ones; the second side gets one thin argument and no example.
- **Errors** fall into three patterns: articles, collocations and memorised template phrases. Every error from the mock is in the error log at the end.

**Reading.** Seven wrong answers at 33/40. Lesson 1 sorts them by question type and cause, so training starts where the marks are lost.

**Speaking.** The top priority, and the only skill with no data. Lesson 1 opens with a recorded baseline mock, and the speaking lessons are tuned to what it shows.

## Targets and the math

Overall 8 needs a four-skill average of 7.75, because IELTS rounds .75 up to the next band. With listening at 8.5, reading, writing and speaking must add up to 22.5 — so overall 8 does not need Reading 9.

| Route | Reading | Writing | Speaking | Overall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 8 | 7 | 7.5 | 8 |
| B | 8.5 | 7 | 7 | 8 |
| C | 7.5 | 7.5 | 7.5 | 8 |
| D | 9 | 7.5 | 8 | 8.5 |
| E | 8.5 | 8 | 8 | 8.5 |

Every route assumes listening holds at 8.5. If it slips to 8 on the day, each route needs one more half band, so the plan aims above the minimum. Reading 9 is the road to 8.5.

**Checkpoints.** Two checkpoints decide whether Month 1 worked. A missed one moves lesson time to that skill in Month 2.

| Skill | Week 2 (23–24 Oct) | Month end (5–6 Nov) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaking | Half a band above baseline | 7–7.5 |
| Writing | 7 on at least one task | 7 on both tasks, timed |
| Reading | 35+/40 | 36–37/40 |
| Listening | 36+/40 | 36+/40 |

## How the month works

Lesson time goes to what needs a live partner: speaking. Writing and reading volume happens at home, and every lesson reviews it.

**Weekly rhythm.** Lessons are on Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

| Day | What happens | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Lesson + homework | 1.5 h + 1.5 h |
| Tue | Self-study | 3 h |
| Wed | Lesson + homework | 1.5 h + 1.5 h |
| Thu | Self-study | 3 h |
| Fri | Lesson + homework | 1.5 h + 1.5 h |
| Sat | Test day | 3 h |
| Sun | Off | 0 |

**Every lesson, 90 minutes,** unless the day says otherwise:

1. **Speaking warm-up, 10 min.** Part 1 rapid round; every error goes into the log.
2. **Speaking core, 35 min.** The day's technique, live practice, feedback, then a redo.
3. **Writing, 35 min.** Review of the last marked essay (15), then the day's technique (20).
4. **Reading clinic, 10 min.** One question type, picked from homework errors.

**Self-study day, 3 hours.** Four blocks, the same every time:

- **Speak, 50 min.** 10 min shadowing; two Part 2 cards recorded (1 min notes, 2 min talk); four Part 3 answers recorded; listen back and log three errors per recording.
- **Write, 70 min.** One timed piece, plus a rewrite of the sentences flagged in the last feedback.
- **Read, 45 min.** One passage in 20 min, 15 min of error analysis, 10 min logging the paraphrases you missed.
- **Fix, 15 min.** Drill from the error log: rewrite each logged sentence correctly from memory.

**Lesson-day homework, 1.5 hours:** rewrite whatever was marked in the lesson (30), re-record the lesson's speaking questions (20), one reading passage with analysis (30), error log (10).

**Test day, about 3 hours:** timed writing test (60), timed reading test (60), recorded speaking self-mock and review (30), error log (15). On 24 Oct and 5 Nov it becomes a full mock in exam order, listening included.

**Listening** gets no lesson time. The two full mocks only check that it holds at 8.5.

**Rules**

1. Every piece of writing is marked within 24 hours and rewritten. Unmarked writing doesn't count.
2. All speaking homework is recorded. Listening back is half the exercise.
3. Timed means timed: phone in another room, no dictionary.
4. The error log is updated every day.
5. On a bad day the minimum is 45 minutes: one Part 2 recording, one reading passage, 10 minutes of error log. Never zero.
6. Sunday is off. Fatigue costs more than one rest day.

## Week 1 — Foundations (12–18 Oct)

Get the speaking baseline, fix the structure of both writing tasks, and start the error log.

### Mon 12 Oct — Lesson 1: Baseline

- **0:00–0:15 Speaking baseline mock.** Recorded, exam conditions, questions below.
- **0:15–0:30 Speaking debrief.** Score yourself on the four criteria first, then compare with the teacher's score. Agree on the three biggest problems.
- **0:30–1:00 Writing debrief.** Go through the mock Task 1 and Task 2 against the error log. Rebuild the overview and the missing conclusion together.
- **1:00–1:20 Reading debrief.** Label each of the seven wrong answers by question type and cause: missed paraphrase, False vs Not Given, time, or careless.
- **1:20–1:30 The plan.** Targets, the math, the rules.

**Baseline mock questions**

- **Part 1.** Work: What do you do? What do you enjoy most about it? Is there anything you'd change about your job? · Mornings: Are you a morning person? What do you do first thing in the morning? Has your morning routine changed over the years? · Weather: What kind of weather do you like most? Does the weather affect your mood?
- **Part 2.** Describe a skill that took you a long time to learn. You should say: what the skill is, when and how you learned it, what made it difficult, and explain how you feel about it now.
- **Part 3.** Which skills matter most for young people today? Is it better to learn a skill from a teacher or online on your own? Why do some adults stop learning new things? How might the skills people need change in the next 20 years? Should schools teach more practical skills and fewer academic subjects?

**Homework, 1.5 h**

- [ ] Rewrite the mock Task 2 with no time limit: add a conclusion, refocus body paragraph 1 on *long* holidays, add one concrete example (45 min)
- [ ] Rewrite the Beechwood Farm overview so it names the two or three biggest changes (10 min)
- [ ] Transcribe two minutes of your Part 2 recording and mark every error and filler (25 min)
- [ ] Error log (10 min)

### Tue 13 Oct — Self-study

- [ ] **Speak:** shadowing; Part 2: a person you admire, an object you use every day; record ten Part 1 answers of two or three sentences each

- [ ] **Read:** one True/False/Not Given passage in 20 min, then analysis

### Wed 14 Oct — Lesson 2: Part 2 and essay structure

- **Warm-up, 10 min.** Part 1: hometown, free time.
- **Speaking core, 35 min.** The Part 2 notes method: five keywords in one minute, then a simple arc — setting, what happened, one vivid detail, how it changed you. Cards: a person who influenced your career; a time you were extremely busy. Feedback, then redo the weaker one.
- **Writing, 35 min.** Feedback on P1. Technique: answer the exact question — underline the key words, plan in five minutes, state the position in the introduction, restate it in the conclusion. Drill: plan three new questions in five minutes each.
- **Reading clinic, 10 min.** False vs Not Given: the decision rule.

**Homework, 1.5 h**

- [ ] Rewrite P1 using the plan method (30 min)
- [ ] Re-record today's two Part 2 cards (20 min)
- [ ] One True/False/Not Given passage with analysis (30 min)
- [ ] Error log (10 min)

### Thu 15 Oct — Self-study

- [ ] **Speak:** shadowing; Part 2: a place you go to relax, a celebration you enjoyed; record four Part 3 answers on work — your before sample for Friday
- [ ] **Write:** Task 1 map in 20 min (a new map from the course or the Cambridge books); Task 2 P2 in 40 min
- [ ] **Read:** one matching-headings passage in 20 min, then analysis
- [ ] **Fix:** collocations from the error log

### Fri 16 Oct — Lesson 3: Part 3 and Task 1 maps

- **Warm-up, 10 min.** Part 1: reading, music.
- **Speaking core, 35 min.** The Part 3 answer shape: direct answer, reason, example, then a contrast or concession. Language for comparing past and present and for speculating. Six live questions on work and cities; redo the weakest two and compare with Thursday's recordings.
- **Writing, 35 min.** Task 1 maps: overview first, then what changed versus what stayed — not compass points. Map verbs: was replaced by, was converted into, was relocated to, made way for, remained unchanged. Review Thursday's map.
- **Reading clinic, 10 min.** Matching headings.

**Homework, 1.5 h**
